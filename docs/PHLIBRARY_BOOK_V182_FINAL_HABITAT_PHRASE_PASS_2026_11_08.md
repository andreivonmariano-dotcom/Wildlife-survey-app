## Request and editorial decision

At the owner's direction, resolve the final 17 species account uses of “associated with” remaining from the 715-account v1.81 habitat expansion. The revision replaces generic associations with cohesive habitat descriptions and rewrites necessary taxonomic, historical-name and feeding-mechanism phrases in more direct language. ## Specific editorial changes

Seventeen accounts changed:
`Dendrocygna arcuata`, `Cypsiurus balasiensis`, `Zosterops japonicus`, `Ansonia mcgregori`, `Platymantis jagorii`, `Limnonectes visayanus`, `Kaloula pulchra`, `Indosylvirana nicobariensis`, `Sanguirana everetti`, `Dibamus leucurus`, `Emydocephalus annulatus`, `Hydrophis platurus`, `Cerberus schneiderii`, `Fordonia leucobalia`, `Brachymeles paeforum`, `Tropidophorus grayi`, and `Dermochelys coriacea`.

- Forest, freshwater and estuarine habitat descriptions now use actual forest-floor substrates, water flow, mud, roots, vegetation and tidal features already supported or consistent with each account.
- The pelagic `Hydrophis platurus` narrative no longer assigns reef slopes, seabed cavities or sand channels as its typical foraging setting. Published physiological/ecological description: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30779371/
- The `Dermochelys coriacea` narrative now concentrates on open-ocean foraging for gelatinous prey and nesting on sandy tropical beaches without generalizing shallow-reef prey. NOAA habitat account: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/leatherback-turtle
- `Emydocephalus annulatus` keeps its fish-egg scraping specialization and coral-reef egg surfaces instead of broad generic hunting lanes. Smithsonian: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/reptiles/turtle-or-sea-snake
- A small number of defensive sentences in these 17 accounts were shortened without deleting material tentative taxonomy or historical-record distinctions.
