# APO | Evidence-based design and development reference
Version 1.0 · 27 November 2026 (Asia/Manila)
Status: research companion to approved Charter v1.3 and Design and Layout Playbook v1.0. It does not amend the charter or certify an APK.

## Executive decision
APO should feel like a natural-history field guide and a practical field notebook. Default screens must be approachable to a learner without GIS, database or software expertise. Professional survey requirements remain intact. The strongest design direction is familiar navigation, a clear visual hierarchy, task-specific forms, discoverable detail and durable local storage. Keep the existing green identity and design tokens until an actual visual comparison supports a change.

## Research method and evidence boundaries
This targeted narrative review used public HCI research, official Android and W3C standards, biodiversity literature, and official competitor documentation. Searches covered touch ergonomics, aesthetic perception, form usability, mobile navigation, visual composition, accessibility, data collection, offline workflows, architecture and evaluation. Relevant passages were opened and checked; source access is identified below. This is not a systematic review or a test of installed competitor apps. No participant recruitment, live APO rendering, new app build or physical-device testing occurred. Recommendations are hypotheses and operational defaults until validated. The existing charter controls scope and scientific meaning.

## 1. Empirical evidence
E01. Parhi, Karlson and Bederson (2006), MobileHCI, Target Size Study for One-Handed Thumb Use on Small Touchscreen Devices. PDF text and pp. 203 and 209 inspected. Two experiments compared discrete and sequential thumb taps. The authors recommended approximately 9.2 mm for discrete and 9.6 mm for serial targets. Older small hardware and controlled tasks limit direct transfer. APO: use the charter's 48 dp floor and test larger repeated count/save controls with both hands; physical millimeters and dp are not interchangeable. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/01/parhi-mobileHCI06.pdf
E02. Tuch et al. (2012), International Journal of Human-Computer Studies 70, 794–811, The role of visual complexity and prototypicality regarding first impression of websites. Author-hosted abstract reviewed. Two screenshot experiments found that familiar, less complex sites received favorable first-impression ratings; this measured aesthetic appraisal, not field-task performance. APO: recognizable layout, restrained surfaces and consistent visual hierarchy; do not infer a universal optimum number of elements. https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/
E03. Seckler et al. (2014), CHI, 1275–1284, Designing Usable Web Forms. PDF methods/discussion text reviewed; screenshot retrieval failed. Controlled eye-tracking experiment (65 participants) applied 20 improvements to three newspaper registration forms. Completion times improved by 18–33% in these forms; the authors emphasized clear input formats and error messages. The intervention was bundled and lab-based, so the effect cannot be attributed to one rule or projected onto APO. APO: label fields persistently, explain units before entry, show validation near the input, retain responses after errors, and measure completion rather than promise a percentage improvement. https://doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557265 ; https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/pdf/42513.pdf
E04. Pernice and Budiu (2016), NN/g, How to Make Navigation (Even a Hamburger) Discoverable on Mobile. Study description and guidance reviewed: 179 users performed tasks on six websites across desktop and phone. Hidden navigation can hinder discovery; effects vary by design. APO: keep four primary destinations labeled and visible where appropriate; test whether people find My Data without instruction. This is evidence from websites, not an APO experiment. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-mobile-even-hamburger/
E05. BioScience (2019), Using Semistructured Surveys to Improve Citizen Science Data for Monitoring Biodiversity. Relevant full-text sections on the observation process and survey effort reviewed. Information about observer, duration, distance, detection and completeness supports analysis of observation bias. Automatic time and location can reduce recording burden. APO: preserve protocol-dependent effort, observer identity, explicit completeness and uncertainty. A complete checklist's nondetection remains distinct from true absence; opportunistic records remain separate. https://academic.oup.com/bioscience/article/69/3/170/5382641
E06. Truong and van der Wal (2024), BioScience 74, 601–613, Exploring the landscape of automated species identification apps. Abstract and introductory full-text passages reviewed. The review describes divergent experiences and data practices across identification apps. APO: keep discovery and scientific recording distinct; no automatic ID, AI model or public upload is authorized by this research. https://doi.org/10.1093/biosci/biae077

## 2. Design standards and applied principles
S01. Android Content composition and structure: compact 16 dp margins are a starting point; align content to a flexible grid, use whitespace and typography for grouping, and adapt columns to width. APO: start with one readable column, use modular galleries only for equal items, use list-detail when wider. https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/content-structure
S02. Android Layouts and navigation patterns: bottom navigation accommodates three to five peer destinations, with rails for larger layouts. One prominent action at a time. APO: use Fieldwork, Atlas, Map and Research Library as primary destinations; preserve active survey state and a visible end action. https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/layout-and-nav-patterns
S03. Android Accessibility: scalable text, contrast, accessible descriptions, alternative actions to gestures and 48 dp touch targets. APO: apply these to custom Views and test actual target bounds; labels must remain readable and focus order logical. https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/foundations/accessibility
S04. W3C WCAG 2.2: 1.4.3 minimum contrast 4.5:1 normal text and 3:1 large text; 1.4.4 supports 200% text enlargement. Apply as accessibility benchmarks in a native app alongside Android guidance, not as a claim of blanket web conformance. Functional visual boundaries should meet applicable 3:1 non-text contrast guidance. https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
S05. Android Color: semantic role tokens, consistent colors, accessible tone differences and light/dark schemes. APO: forest green for primary action, neutral reading surfaces, functional colors with text or shape; test real contrast combinations. A green palette alone does not establish accessibility. https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/styles/color
S06. NN/g Progressive Disclosure: prioritize frequent functions; use clear paths to secondary functions and limit nested disclosure. APO: show effort, count, species and required observations in the recorder; place infrequent diagnostics and optional attributes in named details. Keep survey completion, meaningful uncertainty and mandatory inputs accessible. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/
S07. NN/g 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX: scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast and perceptual grouping. APO: one strong heading and main action, aligned margins, visual proximity, restrained borders and consistent type. This is professional guidance, not a comparative APO trial. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/
S08. NN/g Icon Usability: icon interpretation depends on experience and familiar conventions. APO: label unfamiliar actions and distinguish favorites, bookmarks and saved observations; no gesture-only required action. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/
S09. GOV.UK Question pages: ask only necessary questions, identify optional input, accept uncertainty where valid. Transfer selectively: a guided setup can use steps; repeated field recording should avoid a wizard per sighting. https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/
S10. NN/g Usability Testing 101: realistic tasks, representative participants and neutral facilitation. APO: compare completion, assistance, errors and perceived ease before claiming improvement. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/
S11. Android offline-first architecture: persistent local source of truth; writes to critical data can occur locally before eventual synchronization. APO requires all core reads and writes offline, without a network layer in the present scope. https://developer.android.com/topic/architecture/data-layer/offline-first
S12. Android Save UI states: restore state after recreation and process death; combine transient state with persistent storage where appropriate. APO: survey drafts must survive process death, while transient scroll and filter state may use lighter mechanisms. https://developer.android.com/topic/libraries/architecture/saving-states
S13. Android Guide to app architecture: separation of responsibilities and clear dependencies. APO: extract recorder presentation and state logic in small steps while retaining package, database, methods and historical data. No framework migration is approved. https://developer.android.com/topic/architecture
S14. Android Macrobenchmark: measure startup, scrolling and substantial interactions. APO: measure charter targets on specified devices, editions and repeatable datasets; report median and p95 with trial counts. https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/benchmarking/macrobenchmark-overview
S15. Android Core app quality guidelines: familiar interactions and adaptive/accessibility testing support consistent quality. Adopt relevant native practices incrementally and validate existing Java Views. https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/core-app-quality

## 3. Comparative workflow review
C01. Merlin Bird ID (Cornell Lab): official article explains guided ID, photos, sounds, regional search and galleries. APO should offer meaningful browse routes and an attractive species page with identification first. Do not inherit required pack downloads or assume Philippine automated-ID coverage. https://www.allaboutbirds.org/news/get-more-from-merlin-bird-id-with-these-powerful-features/
C02. eBird Mobile: official instructions show quick checklist entry, common/scientific name lookup, optional detail on sightings, tracking and end review. Adopt low-friction entry, explicit review and effort capture. Preserve APO's independent completion rules and local backups; eBird's account and upload workflow is outside APO's offline scope. https://support.ebird.org/en/support/solutions/articles/48000957940-enter-sightings-with-ebird-mobile
C03. Seek and iNaturalist: official comparison distinguishes private, account-free exploration from a community observation system. Adopt easy entry and local privacy; keep educational activities separate from survey evidence. The individual iNaturalist observation tutorial was returned in search but direct opening failed, so detailed current iNaturalist UI claims are excluded. https://help.inaturalist.org/en/support/solutions/articles/151000169914-what-is-the-difference-between-inaturalist-and-seek-by-inaturalist-
C04. Gaia GPS: official offline map guide explains map coverage, layer selection and resolution. Adopt a map-centered interface and understandable layer controls. APO must ship necessary reference layers; Gaia's subscription/account/download model is incompatible with current scope. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047131513-Download-Maps-for-Offline-Use
C05. ODK Collect: documentation describes drafts, constraints, finalization and recovery after forced quit. Adopt durable drafts, explicit validation, honest completion and interruption recovery; no mandatory server or form-import workflow. https://docs.getodk.org/collect-forms/ ; https://docs.getodk.org/collect-filling-forms/

## 5. Practical layout reference
Use existing design tokens initially. For a new compact screen, start with a 16 dp gutter, 8–12 dp within related content and 24 dp between major sections. Body text around 15–16 sp, secondary text around 13–14 sp, page title around 23–28 sp are project starting ranges, not universal scientific thresholds. Prefer intrinsic height and line wrapping, native fonts and visible focus. Use meaningful photographic focal points with approved source media. No ornamental dashboard, gratuitous gradient, obligatory tutorial or full-screen animation before a core task. Motion should clarify state and respect accessibility.

Home: one obvious Start or Resume Survey, clear Atlas/Map/Library navigation, recent project when useful, optionally one featured species. Avoid competing navigation or duplicated actions.
Fieldwork setup: project and method, then method-required inputs; reuse explicitly confirmed site and preferences. Display selected method requirements clearly; preserve back navigation and every saved input. Do not ask irrelevant fields.
Recorder: compact session status, species entry, count and required detection information, optional method detail, persistent Save/Review/End. Test large targets for repeated taps. Show durable save feedback and recoverable error. Keep essential effort, uncertainty and state visible.
Atlas list: search and filters near the top, full browsable list, common and scientific names, optional approved photo. Keep filters and scroll on returning from a species. Distinguish no results, incomplete reference coverage and unavailable data.
Species detail: approved photo/name and concise identification, habitat and documented distribution first; separate dated conservation authorities. Place deeper taxonomy, bibliography and evidence links below. Preserve scientific names and qualifications. Use real species media or neutral placeholder.
Map: map occupies available area, concise labeled layer control, legend and selected-locality pane. Keep source/resolution accessible. Sensitive locations stay local and deliberate export controls remain.
Research: readable citation list with title, author, year and actual full-text availability; local search, save and open permitted text. My Data: history, survey status, backup, export, and a clear return to active survey. These are reference patterns, not new feature authorizations.

## 6. Test protocol for every substantial redesign
Prepare an exact source and signed-build receipt, identical test dataset, viewport/font/theme settings and before/after captures. Use emulators for repeatable 320 dp and standard phone layouts, 100% and 200% text, keyboard open, rotation, process recreation, permission denial and airplane mode. Exercise screen-reader focus and actions. On physical devices test safe stationary use under sunlight, dim light, one-handed use and weak GNSS; do not simulate hazardous field conditions. Test both handedness and representative low-spec devices. Inspect actual click bounds and semantic roles, not only minimum-height declarations.

Recruit a small formative round of representative Philippine field biologists/technicians and learners, for example 3–5 per audience where feasible. This is a proposed practical starting sample, not a statistical guarantee. Use neutral tasks: resume interrupted survey; log an identified and an unidentified animal; correct a saved count; end early and review status; find an unfamiliar species through browsing; inspect a source; find a saved site; open an offline paper; export or restore a test backup. Record unassisted completion, errors, assistance, time, ease rating and observed confusion. Record actual participant/device counts, limitations and individual failures; avoid pooling incomparable tasks. Compare baseline and candidate with the same tasks and counterbalanced order where possible.

Blocking defects include any data loss, duplicate save, invalid Complete outcome, inaccessible End Survey, hidden required input, sensitive locality disclosure, essential action unavailable offline, unapproved scientific copy or unsupported claims, and layout clipping that prevents essential interaction. Other defects are triaged by task impact and frequency. Aesthetic review considers hierarchy, alignment, spacing, contrast, legibility, consistency and purposeful imagery; record a specific issue, location and correction. Do not invent a numerical design score or a user-test result.

## 7. Implementation order and handoff
First, inventory existing routes/screens and protect survey behavior with tests. Second, repair proven target-size, browse and duplicated-component defects in bounded changes. Third, reduce recorder complexity and evaluate the live capture workflow. Fourth, align Atlas/Map/Library visual language and run representative tasks. Fifth, benchmark and test exact signed upgrades and exports before release. Each change needs an issue or task, baseline, rationale, evidence, source revision, actual result, limitations and direct downloadable artifact if a build exists. No claim of release based on a plan, screenshot, commit, or compilation alone.

The existing charter v1.3 remains authoritative. This reference adds evidence and actionable guidance, without a new charter amendment. The reviewed sources support design decisions, but only actual APO testing can validate the final implementation.
