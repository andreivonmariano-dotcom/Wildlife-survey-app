# LIKAS | Verified reference leads for 12 unresolved Identification sections

**Date:** 2026-10-29. **Status:** Research queue. These leads are **not yet Android app facts** and do not reduce the RC21 count of 12 unsupported Identification accounts. They require exact per-species source examination and cross-taxonomy confirmation.

## Strong next source candidates

- **Fejervarya vittigera.** Sanguila, Labaja, Bernstein & Brown (2026), *An updated herpetological inventory of the Agusan Marsh Wildlife Sanctuary, eastern Mindanao Island, Philippines*, *ZooKeys* and associated Zenodo taxonomic entry. The published **Identification** section provides a character *combination*: olive-grey dorsum, sometimes a pale vertebral line (absent in most), yellow barred inner hindlimbs/groin, smooth dorsal surface of digits, and no flap on tarsus/fifth toe compared with *F. moodiei*. Text and specimens: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12993478/ and https://zenodo.org/records/18921111. This is specimen/site-context attribution, not a province-wide distribution assumption.
- **Trimeresurus schultzei.** Leviton et al. (2019), *Illustrated Key to the Snakes of the Philippines*, genus *Trimeresurus* key. It separates *T. schultzei* with a tail differing strongly in living colour (red tail, green body) and spinose hemipenes; other Philippine congeners lack those combined states. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336170835_Illustrated_Key_to_the_Snakes_of_the_Philippines. The Philippine Red List volume independently describes black reticulation, yellow spots and a reddish tail with a white stripe, https://www.biodiversity.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/PRLC-Book-vertebrates.pdf. Need cite exact key/page and avoid instructing untrained observers to handle venomous snakes for hemipenial verification.
- **Pelophryne albotaeniata.** Barbour 1938 holotype and Inger 1954 account listed by AMNH: https://amphibiansoftheworld.amnh.org/Amphibia/Anura/Bufonidae/Pelophryne/Pelophryne-albotaeniata. A 2017 National Museum of the Philippines report says two white dorsolateral lines, traditionally used as identification characters, vary and may reflect sex/ontogeny/population variation: https://www.finsandleaves.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/NotesontheforestfrogsofCleopatrasNeedleMountainRange2017Conslidated.pdf. Character should be treated as variable, not a unique fixed diagnostic.
- **Platymantis hazelae / P. isarog / P. banahao.** Brown, Brown & Alcala (1997) *Species of the hazelae group … with descriptions of two new species*, *Proceedings of California Academy of Sciences* 49:405–421. https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/part/52974. Primary taxonomic text must be read separately for each species before adding an Identification section. Generic *P. hazelae*-group phalangeal and finger-disc characters **do not distinguish species**. See https://eol.org/pages/1019298/articles.
- **Limnonectes acanthi.** AMNH nomenclature and geographic account: https://amphibiansoftheworld.amnh.org/Amphibia/Anura/Dicroglossidae/Dicroglossinae/Limnonectes/Limnonectes-acanthi. Herr et al. (2021) separate *L. beloncioi* from previously included *L. acanthi*-like Mindoro/Semirara frogs by call and molecular evidence, not reliable morphology alone. Do not use type locality as an Identification trait.
- **Gekko gigante.** Brown & Alcala (1978) original Philippine gekkonid monograph is listed by Reptile Database but that record leaves Diagnosis blank: https://reptile-database.reptarium.cz/Gekko/gigante. A distribution/type specimen listing is not sufficient to create diagnostic claims.
